# Audit: holes in the k-means production write-up

Holes in the logic of parts 1–4 of [`kmeans_in_production.ipynb`](kmeans_in_production.ipynb), found on 2026-11-06 after
part 5 (the code) was built. Ordered by how much each could change a decision. Items 6, 10 and 14 were proved by
part 5's runs rather than argued. Tick the status column as they get plugged; resolved items are removed and the
numbers of the rest stay as they were.

| # | where | the hole | plug | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | the alarm rule | "e.g. 3 of the last 5" with no way to choose m and n. The false-alarm / detection-delay table and the cost trade-off exist only in part 5's assumptions. | Put the method in part 1: false-alarm budget across all signals, delay for a clear change, replay on past healthy weeks. | open |
| 5 | alarm lifecycle | Nothing says when an alarm closes or what it suppresses while open. After "yes, no" or "not real", the old model stays live while its bands keep breaching, and part 1's re-draw rule doesn't list these outcomes, so the alarm never closes. | Define open/close: an alarm closes on adopt, roll back, rebaseline, or "fix the data"; "yes, no" and "not real" rebaseline the old model like an accepted mix shift. | open |
| 6 | step 3 | "Fit recovered" and "far points gone" are judged against the *new* model's band and cut-off, which were just built from the same data: the far share is ~1% by construction. The hidden-shapes check also fires when k-means merely splits a skewed cluster (5.7, 5.8). The curve (5.9) passed all three; only the undocumented hollow check caught it. | Judge against the *old* cut-off and band, or on later batches; add the hollow check; say how to tell a skew-split from a shape. | open |
| 7 | step 4, "real?" | Only a consistency test. The write-up itself says big data makes any gain win on every subsample, so "real" is nearly always yes at scale and the whole weight falls on the holdout. "Reorganisation ⇒ not real" also presumes the old structure is right. | A minimum silhouette gain (like the PSI practical minimum); a path for a real, meaningful reorganisation. | open |
| 8 | step 4 scenarios | "No: Black Friday … two weeks later it is gone" cannot be known at `t_alarm`. Under retrain-at-the-alarm a transient passes "real?" inside the alarm window and is caught later by the holdout ("yes, no") or by the rollback. | Move the transient to those two paths. | open |
| 9 | between alarm and switch | "Retrain at the alarm" but adoption waits for a weeks-long holdout. Nothing says what is live meanwhile, what to do with customers the old model demonstrably misassigns (the far points), or what happens if a second change arrives. | State it: old model live and flagged; far points into an "unassigned, no campaign" bucket; a second alarm restarts step 1. | open |
| 10 | step 6 | No sample size, duration or minimum detectable lift, and no way for a new or split segment to *get* a campaign to test. Part 5 needed a pilot and 8 weeks of customers before any lift was visible. | Pilot for new segments, inherit for unchanged ones; a power line (customers per arm for the lift you care about). | open |
| 11 | step 5 vs 6 | Does a same-k replacement need the holdout? Step 5 ends in "replace", step 6 says "validate before switching"; it can't be both for a silent weekly replacement. Also unstated: shift and fit gain are measured in which scaled space, given step 2 refits the scaler. | Holdout for visible replacements only; measure in the old model's scaled space. | open |
| 12 | the bands | Random draws of the held-out set give sampling noise only. Real weeks also vary for real reasons (paydays, seasons), so healthy weeks will breach; the sliding reference is mentioned but no replay of the bands on past healthy weeks is required before go-live. | Replay the band + rule on the training year's weeks; widen or use seasonal references where they breach. | open |
| 13 | the held-out split | Rows, not customers: a customer appears in many weeks, so held-out rows share customers with training rows and the bands come out too tight. "One row per customer" is said per batch, not for the split. | Split by customer ID. | open |
| 14 | step 2 / preprocessing | Standardise and PCA are named; log transforms are not. The features in the shop are lognormal, and the spurious dormant splits in 5.7 and 5.10 are skew, not structure. | Transform skewed features before scaling; store the transform with the model. | open |
| 15 | concept drift | The row jumps to "new features, then re-cluster". The cheaper first response, same segments with a re-piloted best action, is missing. | Add "re-pilot the campaign per segment" before "new feature". | open |
| 16 | the business metric | Each segment's outcome is stored at go-live and then left to "the teams". It is a rate per segment, so the same band and m-of-n rule can watch it (5.11 computed it weekly). | Make it a signal with a band, reported to the teams rather than discovered by them. | open |
| 17 | upstream feature change | One row for added / removed / redefined / rescaled. Added leaves the old model valid; removed breaks scoring; rescaled may need a transform, not a retrain. No interim action is given for the weeks the old model cannot run. | Split the row; name the interim (last valid assignments, or a rule-based fallback). | open |
| 18 | go-live store | The list omits the scaler, PCA, the transforms, the feature definitions (the data contract the "redefined" row relies on) and the code version. | Add them to the model version. | open |

Suggested order: 5 and 6 first, since they change what the playbook decides; the rest are completeness.
